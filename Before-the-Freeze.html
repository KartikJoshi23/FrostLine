Before the Freeze

Money from crime is often moved as digital dollars. The company behind those dollars can block a wallet, but the block usually comes late. We are designing an early-warning tool. It warns a person when a wallet starts to look like wallets that were blocked in the past. And it is open about how often it is wrong.

The problem

Digital dollars move in minutes. A block often comes after a week or more. By then, much of the money has left.

Our answer

Study wallets that were blocked in the past. Learn the early signs. Give a warning when the same signs appear again.

What is new

Others give early warnings too. We add six things: before the vote, honest, fair, clear, hard to trick, and tested live.

First, six simple words

If you know these six words, you can understand everything else.

USDT

A digital dollar. 1 USDT is meant to be worth 1 US dollar.

Tether

The company that makes USDT.

TRON

A public record book on the internet. Anyone can read the USDT payments in it.

Wallet

Like a bank account number, but with no name on it.

Freeze

Tether blocks a wallet. Its USDT can no longer be sent.

Public vote

Before a freeze, two of three key holders must say yes. Anyone can see the first request.

Remember: a freeze is a decision by Tether. It is not a court saying that someone is guilty.

The problem

In one lineTether can block a wallet, but the block usually comes late. By then, much of the money has left.

Here is a made-up story that shows how it goes wrong. Follow the arrows, or press Next.

Why is this so hard to fix?

3 in 4

The block comes late

About three in four frozen wallets were first seen more than a week before their freeze. That is plenty of time to move money.

$26,040 and $514

Today's tools can fool themselves

In one dataset, a typical frozen wallet moves $26,040 at a time, and a normal one $514. Telling them apart is as easy as telling an elephant from a mouse. It does not prove that a tool can see trouble coming.

70 of 133

Honest people can get hurt

Strangers can drop junk into your wallet. We found payments from copycat senders in 70 of 133 wallets we checked. In August 2026 the exchange Kraken said nearly 12,000 tiny payments led to limits on its customers.

The question we want to answer

Can we warn people before the public vote, be honest about our mistakes, and be hard to trick?

We split it into five small questions

  1. Do wallets behave differently before they get frozen?
  2. How many days earlier can we see it?
  3. Do frozen wallets work together in groups?
  4. How many freezes happen only because of official lists?
  5. How much money reaches a cash-out place before the freeze?

Why does it matter? Exchanges, supervisors and police could look sooner. A paper by UAE supervisors already asks firms to make rules for USDT on TRON and to watch for freezes. We test how early such rules can work.

Part 3 of 8

The solution

In one lineWe build a forecast for wallets, the way a weather service builds a forecast for storms.

A weather service

  1. Studies past storms.
  2. Learns the signs that came before them.
  3. Warns you when it sees the same signs.
  4. Tells you how often it is wrong.

Our tool

  1. Studies wallets that were frozen in the past.
  2. Learns the signs that came before the freeze.
  3. Warns a person when it sees the same signs.
  4. Tells how often it is wrong.

A forecast is not proof of a storm. In the same way, our warning is not proof of a crime. It only says: this wallet looks like wallets that were frozen later, so please take a look.

The ten rules we lock before testing

Step 8 in the chart says "lock our rules". These are the rules. We write them down, seal them with a date (how?), and only then compute a test result.

Who decided these rules, and where do they come from?

The four of us proposed them. But we did not invent them freely. Every rule comes from one or more of three places, and each card below says which.

Published work

A paper, a regulator or an existing tool already uses it or advises it.

Our own count

We measured it in public data.

Our choice

We found no source that gives a number, so we picked one. Where it could change a result, we also show other numbers.

They become final when we lock them, before any test result exists. They can be reviewed before that. A rule that changes later is listed openly as changed.

  1. Which wallets countOnly a wallet's first freeze. Wallets with fewer than 3 real payments, or silent for over 30 days, are set aside and counted openly. Very busy wallets, such as exchanges, are left out.
    Published workOur own countOur choice

    Where it comes from: FreezeCheck also needs at least 3 payments. We count the first freeze only, because we found 36 wallets that were frozen more than once. Very busy is our choice: over 1,000 contacts in 90 days, or over 5,000 payments in 30 days. We repeat with half and with double.

  2. Which day we stand onEight moments before the public vote: 90, 60, 30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 days, and 1 hour.
    Published workOur choice

    Where it comes from: The finish line is Tether's own public vote. Wu et al. (2026) also record that moment. The eight moments are our choice, and we show all eight.

  3. What counts as junkPayments of 0 USDT. Also payments up to 10 USDT from a stranger who has never sent more than 10.
    Published workOur own countOur choice

    Where it comes from: FreezeCheck reports bait payments of 1 to 5 USDT. In our check, all 37 'outgoing' payments of 0 USDT were written by someone else. The limit of 10 is our choice, and we repeat with 1 and 100.

  4. Which clues we useSize, speed, waiting time, senders and receivers, age, round amounts and close neighbours.
    Published work

    Where it comes from: FATF (2026) lists 40 warning signs, by our count. Five can be tested from payments alone, and our clues cover them. The others are simple measures used in earlier work.

  5. Whom we compare withAbout 20 random and 5 similar wallets from the same day, taken from a fixed 1-in-200 sample.
    Published workOur choice

    Where it comes from: Arp et al. (2022) warn against unfair samples. Luo et al. (2023) warn that an unlabelled wallet is not proven normal. The numbers 20, 5 and 1 in 200 are our choice, limited by how much data we can store. We repeat with half as many partners.

  6. Which months teach and testLearn: July 2024 to September 2025. Tune: October to December 2025. Test: January to October 2026.
    Published workOur choice

    Where it comes from: Pendlebury et al. (2019): learn from the past, test on the future. The dates are our choice, with the newest months kept for the test. We repeat with three start dates.

  7. Which models we tryA checklist, logistic regression, decision trees and a neural network. The one with the most right warnings on the Tune months, at 1,000 a month, is kept.
    Published work

    Where it comes from: The checklist is from FATF (2026), logistic regression is used by FreezeCheck, and trees did best in StableAML (2026). The neural network is a topic of our course.

  8. Where the warning line isIt is set on the Tune months, so that about 1,000 wallets a month are above it.
    Our choice

    Where it comes from: It is about 45 warnings on each working day, which a small team can read. We also show the results for 100 and for 500.

  9. What counts as a passFour checks: real, better, useful and strong.
    Published workOur own count

    Where it comes from: '1 in 25' comes from 19 US banks (Bank Policy Institute, 2018). The checklist to beat is from FATF (2026). The easy cases come from Arp et al. (2022) and from our own checks of the data.

  10. What we may say at the endOne of six fixed results, from 'no early warning found' to 'useful early warning'.
    Published workOur choice

    Where it comes from: Hofman et al. (2023) advise to fix in advance how a model will be judged. The six sentences are our own.

AI agents, orchestration and blockchain

AI agents

Three AI helpers explain a result: one finds the facts, one doubts, one writes. They never give a score and never decide. Code checks their numbers, and a person says yes or no.

Orchestration

Dagster is the manager of the data steps. LangGraph is the manager of the AI helpers. If one of the 14 time checks fails, the manager stops everything after it.

Blockchain

TRON gives us the data. Bitcoin is used as a public clock. Before testing, we write down our ten rules (who decided them?) and save the trained model. A fingerprint of both goes onto Bitcoin. Later, it shows that we did not change them after seeing the results. Why not just a dated file?

Part 6 of 8

What is new

In one lineOthers give early warnings too. Ours will also give early warnings. New are the six things we add, so that people can see how far to trust a warning.

Will our tool give early warnings?

Yes. That is its job. It gives a wallet a score, and a warning before the public vote.

On live data too?

Yes, as the last step. First we test it on past months. If it passes, we run a four-week live trial on new TRON payments.

Do others do this already?

Partly. FreezeCheck scores wallets today. The Early Detection team trained a model. So the warning itself is not our new idea.

Then what is new?

How the warning is made and tested. We add the six things below. They come from gaps in past work (how we searched). We found no published tool that has all six.

The six things we add

1

Before the vote

Others: On TRON, one tool looks at a wallet until the freeze, when the vote is already public. Another stops one hour before the freeze. In 2026 the vote was already public by then in about one group in three.

We: stop before the public vote, and go back up to 90 days.

In weather wordsA forecast made after the storm is on the news is not a forecast.

2

Honest

Others: mostly say how many frozen wallets they caught.

We: also count the wrong warnings, for each number of days.

In weather wordsTell people about the false alarms too, not only the storms you caught.

3

Fair

Others: In the Early Detection dataset, big frozen wallets are compared with small normal ones.

We: compare with similar wallets from the same day.

In weather wordsStorm or sunny day is easy. Storm or cloudy day is the real test.

4

Clear

Others: mostly treat every freeze as the same.

We: check which kind the tool has learned: official lists, scams, or freezes that were undone.

In weather wordsDoes it know thunder from snow?

5

Hard to trick

Others: As far as we found, they only take out very tiny payments.

We: measure how much money a stranger needs to cause a false warning.

In weather wordsCan someone with a garden hose set off the rain sensor?

6

Tested live

Others: FreezeCheck says its own test is not a live forecast and was not registered before.

We: lock the rules first. Then we run a live trial on new payments.

In weather wordsWrite down tomorrow's forecast today, then wait.

Our promises

  • We never call a wallet or a person criminal.
  • We never link a wallet to a name.
  • We never publish a warning about a wallet that was not frozen. In the live trial these stay in a sealed list.

Part 7 of 8

Questions people will ask

Short answers. Click a question to open it.

Is a frozen wallet a thief's wallet?

Not always. A freeze is Tether's decision, and we do not say every freeze is right. Some wallets are released later: on 14 May 2026 Tether released 447 in 26 minutes. So we never call a wallet or a person criminal.

Do you predict crime?

No. We look for wallets that resemble wallets Tether froze later. That is likeness to Tether's past decisions, not proof of a crime. With the court cases we also check what the tool has learned beyond that.

Who would use a warning?

An exchange, before it pays out cash. A supervisor, to see how early a firm could have known. Police, to ask Tether sooner. Our project only builds and tests the tool.

Why not just ask Tether to freeze faster?

We cannot change how Tether works. A warning helps others to look earlier, whatever Tether does.

What if a warning is wrong?

It will be, many times. That is why a person decides, why we count wrong warnings, and why the live list stays sealed. The tool never blocks or reports anyone.

Could thieves use this to hide?

We publish no list of weights and no guide. We test tricks that could hurt an honest wallet. We do not test how a thief could hide.

How is this different from FreezeCheck?

FreezeCheck scores wallets today, and says openly that it did not measure wrong flags and that its test was not a live forecast. We stop before the vote, count wrong warnings, test tricks, and try the tool live with locked rules.

Which rules do you lock, and why with Bitcoin?

The ten rules in Part 3, together with the trained model. A date on a file can be changed. A date in Bitcoin cannot. It shows when the rules existed. It does not show that they are good.

Who decided the ten rules?

The four of us proposed them. Each rule comes from published work, from our own count of public data, or is our own choice. Part 3 shows this for every rule. Where our own number could change a result, we also show other numbers. They can be reviewed before the lock, and any later change is listed openly.

Could you pick rules that help your tool?

We lock them before any test result exists, so we cannot know which rule would help. In the live trial even the data does not exist yet.

Have you already seen the test months?

We have counted freezes and read small samples from past months, also from 2026, to understand the problem. We have not trained or tested any model. This is one more reason for the live trial: its weeks lie in the future, so nobody has seen them.

When is a warning right?

When Tether's public vote on that wallet starts within 30 days after it. It is early when the vote starts at least 7 days after it, and the four checks use the early ones. All other warnings count as wrong, even if the wallet is frozen much later. The same rule is used on past months and in the live trial.

Can the AI helpers make things up?

Yes, any AI can. So normal code checks every number against the stored results, and a person approves every note. The helpers never give a score.

What if the coins are fake?

We read one program only: the real USDT. A coin that copies its name cannot enter our data. Freezes are written by that same program, so they cannot be faked either.

What if a stranger plants payments?

This is real. We mark who wrote each payment, take the junk out and test how well that works. The 0.01 USDT filter used in the Early Detection dataset caught only 413 of 1,880 small payments in our sample.

What if a wallet was stolen?

The public record cannot show that. The coins and the signature are real, but the owner lost control. We say so openly.

What if someone uses many wallets?

Each wallet is scored alone, because the record shows no owner. The money map looks for groups of wallets that work together. It can miss some.

Are the comparison wallets really good wallets?

We do not know, and we never call them that. They are only not up for a freeze on that day. We repeat the results as if 0.1% and 1% of them were hidden cases.

Would an exchange's own wallet be flagged?

Very busy wallets are left out on both sides, and we say how many frozen wallets this removes.

Can a laptop score millions of wallets?

Not all of them, and it does not need to. One search in BigQuery first leaves out wallets that show none of the warning clues. Only the rest are scored on the laptop. The same filter is used on the past months.

If the live list is sealed, how does it help anybody?

The trial is a test, not a service. It shows whether such a tool can be trusted. If it can, a firm could run it for itself, in private and with trained people.

Will the live trial end in time?

It takes about two months: four weeks of warnings and then 30 days of waiting. If the course ends earlier, we publish what has come in by then, and say so.

Is this fair to wallet owners?

We use public records only, no names, and we publish no warning about a wallet that was not frozen. The trick test exists to protect honest owners.

What if Tether changes how it works?

We show results for each quarter, so a change becomes visible. In the live trial the newest weeks are used.

What if the tool does not work?

Then we say so. The six results are fixed before testing, and 'no early warning found' is one of them. A tool that fails on past data is not tried live.

What if a data source stops?

The freezes, votes, lists and published datasets are already read or downloaded. If Google's payment data stops, we read the same payments from TronGrid, more slowly.

Is the data private?

All data is public. We load no names, and we tie no wallet to a person.

Why only USDT on TRON?

In one study, nine in ten of the wallets frozen in June 2025 were on TRON, and research on TRON is scarce. The method could be used elsewhere, but we make no claim about that.

What does it cost?

The tools are open and the data is public, so the plan needs no paid service.

Did you check your own numbers?

Yes, twice, with new programs. The second check found mistakes, and we corrected them.

The six gaps, and what we do about each

The gap we found in past workWhat our tool does
1Tests stop too lateBefore the vote. We stop before the public vote, and go back up to 90 days.
2Only the hits are countedHonest. We also count the wrong warnings, for each number of days.
3Unlike wallets are comparedFair. We compare with similar wallets from the same day.
4All freezes are mixedClear. We check which kind the tool has learned: official lists, scams, or freezes that were undone.
5We found no test of tricksHard to trick. We measure how much money a stranger needs to cause a false warning.
6Tests use the past onlyTested live. We lock the rules first. Then we run a live trial on new payments.

A freeze is Tether's decision. Our score shows likeness to wallets frozen in the past. It is not proof of wrongdoing.
